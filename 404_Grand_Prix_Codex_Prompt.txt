Player flow

QR opens /join. Show a title page with society branding, 20 challenges, top 10 prizes and a Start Game button. That button opens a form for name and registration number, then joins a lobby. Explain the scoring before play. The organizer starts everyone together after registration; use five racing lights and a five-second countdown. Lock new registrations at race start.

After the shared start, each player independently answers 20 questions, one at a time. Show four large answer buttons, question number, time remaining, score, rank and connection status. Lock an answer immediately and move to the next question after server acknowledgment. Never wait for a Next button. On timeout, record zero and advance. Finishing opens personal results and live standings.

Exact prize-cutoff ties must be flagged for an organizer-run tie-break.

SERVER RULES AND FAIRNESS
Timing and question order

Questions 1 to 5 get 15 seconds each; 6 to 10 get 20 seconds; 11 to 15 get 25 seconds; 16 to 20 get 30 seconds. Total question allowance is 450 seconds. Set a global race deadline nine minutes after the countdown ends. Shuffle questions only within their five-question group and shuffle options per player. Persist the order and option IDs so refresh cannot change them.

The server controls starts, question issue times and deadlines. Start the first question for all players at the scheduled race start. Issue the next question when the current answer is accepted. After a timeout, the next question starts at the expired deadline, even while the player is disconnected. On reconnect or restart, catch up elapsed questions as timeouts. A client cannot gain time by hiding the tab, delaying requests, refreshing or changing its clock. Load SVGs before the start.

Scoring and results

Give 100 points for a correct answer and zero for incorrect or timed-out answers. Maximum score is 2000. Sort by score descending, then total adjusted answer time ascending. A correct answer contributes server-measured elapsed time; an incorrect answer or timeout contributes its full time allowance. Use milliseconds internally. Exact ties share a competition rank; stable display ordering is not a sporting tie-break. If a tie crosses the top-10 cutoff, flag it and let the organizer settle it before gifts are awarded.

Final prize eligibility requires reaching the end of all 20 questions; timeouts count as completed questions. Display unfinished status separately if the organizer ends the race early. Only the backend may change scores, deadlines or ranks. Accept answers only for the currently issued question, belonging to that player and event, within its server deadline. Never trust submitted score, time or rank.

Persistence and access

Use SQLite with WAL mode, short transactions, prepared statements and appropriate unique constraints. Persist events, participants, per-player question order, issue/deadline timestamps and answers. Normalize registration numbers by trimming and uppercasing; validate length and safe characters without assuming one semester prefix. Enforce one registration per event. Duplicate names are allowed. Validate and limit names, render them as text, and neutralize spreadsheet formulas in CSV exports.

Give each player an unguessable session in a cookie, using HttpOnly and Secure in production. Duplicate registration attempts must not reveal another player session. Reloading in the same browser resumes through its existing cookie. Protect every organizer API and socket operation with server-side authentication. Keep secrets in environment variables. Apply sensible limits to joins, login attempts and answer requests; account for many students sharing one Wi-Fi IP.

Record each answer atomically once using a unique event/player/question key. Save before acknowledging. Retry of the same request returns its saved receipt without adding points or advancing twice. On reconnect always fetch a fresh authoritative snapshot. Broadcast public standings at most once per second. Emit no answer keys, explanations, future questions, private IDs, cookies or registration numbers to public clients.

Short timers, shuffled options and visual puzzles reduce convenient cheating, but do not claim the game is AI-proof. Do not disqualify players merely for tab switching. Reveal answer explanations after the event closes. Winners will have their registration numbers verified against student IDs.

QUESTION BANK ONE
Seed exactly these 20 questions with four options, the stated correct answer and a short explanation. The numbered order defines difficulty groups; options are shuffled at runtime. The seed file and keys must stay on the server.

1. Which planet is known as the Red Planet?
Options: Venus | Mars | Jupiter | Mercury. Correct: Mars.

2. How many months have at least 28 days?
Options: 1 | 2 | 11 | 12. Correct: 12.

3. Which weighs more: 1 kg of iron or 1 kg of cotton?
Options: Iron | Cotton | Equal | Depends on size. Correct: Equal.

4. What day comes two days after Tuesday?
Options: Wednesday | Thursday | Friday | Sunday. Correct: Thursday.

5. Visual: Count only the triangles in this arrangement.
Options: 2 | 3 | 4 | 5. Correct: 3. Draw exactly three triangles, four circles and two squares, without overlap. Vary sizes and rotations.

6. You overtake the runner in second place. What position are you now?
Options: First | Second | Third | Cannot tell. Correct: Second.

7. An electric train travels north; wind blows west. Which direction does its smoke travel?
Options: North | South | West | No smoke. Correct: No smoke.

8. What is 8 + 8 divided by 4? Render as 8 + 8 ÷ 4.
Options: 4 | 10 | 12 | 16. Correct: 10. Division is performed first.

9. A farmer has 17 sheep. All but 9 die. How many remain alive?
Options: 8 | 9 | 17 | 26. Correct: 9.

10. Visual: How many squares are in this complete 2 by 2 grid?
Options: 4 | 5 | 6 | 8. Correct: 5. Draw four equal cells in one square. Count four small squares and one large square.

QUESTION BANK TWO
11. Two fathers and two sons each eat one sandwich. Only three sandwiches are eaten. How many people are there?
Options: 2 | 3 | 4 | 5. Correct: 3. They are a grandfather, father and son.

12. Find the next number: 2, 6, 12, 20, ?
Options: 26 | 28 | 30 | 32. Correct: 30. Differences increase as 4, 6, 8, 10.

13. Visual: An arrow starts pointing up. Rotate it clockwise by 90°, 180°, 90°, then 180°. Where does it point?
Options: Up | Right | Down | Left. Correct: Down. Draw the initial arrow and instructions; do not draw the solved arrow.

14. A bat and ball cost Rs. 110 together. The bat costs Rs. 100 more than the ball. What does the ball cost?
Options: Rs. 5 | Rs. 10 | Rs. 15 | Rs. 20. Correct: Rs. 5.

15. Visual equations: Three circles = 12. One circle + two triangles = 10. One triangle + one square = 7. Find circle + triangle × square.
Options: 16 | 20 | 28 | 40. Correct: 16. Circle = 4, triangle = 3, square = 4. Draw individual identical shapes and clear operators.

16. A snail climbs a 10 m wall. Each day it climbs 3 m; each night it slips 2 m. On which day does it first reach the top?
Options: 7 | 8 | 9 | 10. Correct: Day 8. It reaches the top before slipping again.

17. Visual: How many rectangles, including squares, are in a complete grid with 2 rows and 3 columns?
Options: 6 | 12 | 18 | 24. Correct: 18. Draw all boundaries. There are three choices of horizontal boundary pairs and six vertical pairs.

18. Three boxes contain apples only, oranges only, or both. Their labels are all wrong. From which labeled box should you draw one fruit to identify every box?
Options: Apples | Oranges | Mixed | Any box. Correct: Mixed. The Mixed box must contain only one fruit type.

19. Cards show E, K, 4, 7. Each card has a letter on one side and a number on the other. To test the rule that every vowel has an even number on its reverse, which cards must you flip?
Options: E and 4 | E and 7 | K and 4 | 4 and 7. Correct: E and 7. The odd number must not hide a vowel.

20. Visual: On a grid of 3 rows and 3 columns, how many routes connect the top-left corner to the bottom-right corner along grid lines, moving only right or down?
Options: 6 | 12 | 20 | 24. Correct: 20. Draw nine cells, start and finish at outer corners, and allowed directions. Every route consists of three right and three down moves.
